  The Problem Statement

Early generative computer vision was dominated by Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). While capable of generating sharp images, GANs suffered from notorious training instability, hyperparameter sensitivity, and mode collapse (generating only a narrow subset of training data). Furthermore, conditioning GANs on complex, nuanced natural language descriptions proved brittle. Diffusion models solved these foundational challenges by reformulating image synthesis as a continuous physical process of iterative denoising, establishing a new paradigm for high-fidelity, controllable multimodal generation.

  Theoretical Framework

The Denoising Diffusion Framework (DDPM): Diffusion models operate across two mirrored processes:

  • Forward Process (q): A fixed Markov chain that gradually corrupts a clean image x_0 by adding Gaussian noise over T discrete timesteps according to a variance schedule beta_1, ..., beta_T. At timestep T, the image is indistinguishable from isotropic Gaussian noise.
  • Reverse Process (p_theta): A learned neural network (traditionally a U-Net with attention layers, or a Diffusion Transformer / DiT) trained to predict the exact noise vector added at timestep t, allowing the algorithm to subtract the noise and reconstruct a clean sample.

Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs): Running diffusion directly in high-resolution pixel space (e.g. 1024x1024x3 = 3.1 million values) requires immense computational power. Stable Diffusion revolutionized the field by first training a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) to compress images 8x into a compact latent space (128x128x4), performing the iterative diffusion process entirely within this lower-dimensional manifold, and decoding the final latent back into pixels.

Text Conditioning via Cross-Attention: A pre-trained text encoder (such as CLIP ViT-L or T5) converts text prompts into embedding vectors. Inside the diffusion U-Net, cross-attention layers allow intermediate visual latent features to attend to text tokens, steering the generated content to match the text prompt.

  Implementation Guide

Key parameters and workflows when generating and editing images via diffusion:

  1. Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG Scale): Balances prompt adherence against image diversity. Low CFG (3-5) produces creative, diverse images; moderate CFG (7-9) balances quality and prompt adherence; high CFG (15+) creates oversaturated, hyper-literal renders.
  2. Denoising Sampling Schedulers: Algorithms like DDIM, Euler Ancestral, and DPM++ Solve the reverse diffusion differential equations in 20-30 steps rather than 1,000 steps, cutting generation latency by 97%.
  3. Inpainting & Outpainting: By masking specific pixels in latent space and conditioning the reverse process on unmasked boundaries, models can seamlessly fill in missing elements or expand canvas boundaries.
  4. Negative Prompting: Implemented by calculating guidance away from unwanted terms during classifier-free guidance, suppressing artifacts, blurry details, and undesirable visual motifs.
